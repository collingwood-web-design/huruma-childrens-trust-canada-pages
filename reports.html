Report

Summer 2026

With summer almost here, but some temperatures having felt like August already, I hope all of you are well and making plans for some rest and relaxation during the next couple of months with family and friends. It will be beneficial to have a little calm in these unsettled times. At Huruma the children will have a break from their schooling, which they greatly love, and there will be some opportunity for them to just have fun.

As mentioned in my March 2026 report, I was able to spend some time at Huruma Children's Home in Kenya this spring. It is a “happy place” for me, and I find joy spending time with the children and seeing the difference we are making in their lives and the lives of Mama and the team as well.

The garden at Gatune is thriving, and each growing season they are able to harvest more vegetables and legumes to help reduce the impact of the increasing costs of food and help make the home more resilient to global pressures. They are trying to develop revenue streams to increase income for the home by selling excess produce and clean water to other community members.

More recently in Kenya, there were days of protests because of fuel shortages, increased food costs and costs of gas due to the war in Iran. The financial situation remains dire throughout Kenya and at Huruma. The state of the world does not allow people to be as generous as they have been in the past, leaving fewer donations to meet all of the needs of the children.

Today (June 14th) one Canadian dollar is worth 92.4 Kenyan shillings. Their shilling remains very volatile on the world market. We wanted this note to be a quick update on the children and team at Huruma with our request for you to consider helping the children at Huruma with a donation as you are able. Everybody's situation is different, but we want you to know that any amount will help, and all donations regardless of amount will receive a Canadian charitable tax receipt.

Take care and enjoy the summer,
Donna Mansfield and the Board of Directors of Huruma Children's Trust (Canada)

Report

March 2026 — Huruma visit

I was fortunate to be able to visit Mama Zipporah and her children at Huruma Children's Home in Ngong Hills, Kenya during March 2026. I was greeted by all the children who welcomed me with hugs and big smiles.

Still so many children continue to reside there, and as always, many children come in daily from the local slums for their education and their main meal of the day.

The basketball court needed a bit of work. These nets and metal backboards were purchased and put in place by Canadian volunteers who visited in 2024. Once fixed, pickup games began immediately.

The children loved to show how well they read English books. They would sit quietly beside me taking their time sounding out their words when necessary. Often, little ones would be sitting at their sides listening quietly.

I am also always impressed with the children's memories. We play the memory game with playing cards when kids have to remember where turned-over cards were to match them. They were all attentive, and very successful. In the children's free time, they love to engage with volunteers. I had brought bubbles, paper, crayons and chalk. One activity we did, to hopefully aid in their self-esteem, is we traced their bodies and had them colour them. I titled them “Magnificent” (and name). One little girl coloured hers giving herself a tiara.

We visited the farm at Gatune. Lots of legumes, kale and maize growing and the beehives are quite active, which was great to see as Canadians raised the funds to build them.

They have had lots of rain and the soil smelled so healthy. Along with the ponds, Mama has also had a deep well drilled in the garden. This is providing consistent water for the gardens and also she has a plan to sell water to the locals so they don't have to travel so far for good, clean water. With the support of Hope for Huruma (American-based charity) Mama has also built more housing for the transitional youth that work at the farm learning about agriculture, with the electricity provided by solar panels. She is building a classroom as well so the youth can learn the theory of farming and go immediately to the fields to put their learning into practice.

A lot is happening in Kenya right now. There is legislation that has been approved that mandates that all children living in children's homes will be placed in family-based foster homes by 2032. During my visit I met with government officials and connected with an NGO partnering with Huruma. I gained some insight into potential impacts to the children in Huruma and possibly for Huruma Children's Trust (Canada). We will continue to monitor the impact of these potential changes. There are 40,000 Kenyan children living in 902 children's homes, like Huruma, throughout the country. These next few years will be pivotal to see if that goal can be achieved, as Kenya is expecting international donors to fund the children's needs in the foster homes.

Report

January 2026

We are happy to report that 2025 was another successful year for Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) in support of Huruma Children's Home in Ngong, Kenya.

Through your donations, Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) was able to donate $20,000.00 Canadian dollars over the year to Huruma. At today's rate (January 4, 2026) with 93.90 Kenyan shillings equal to 1.00 Canadian dollar, that equals 1,877,934.27 shillings. Thank you to everyone!

Mama Zipporah and her daughter Sialo are continuing their hard work at running the home and meeting the needs of the 300+ children living there. And as we know, with education being the pathway out of poverty throughout the world, there continues to be 60 children coming from the local slums for their education and main meal of their day. As we mention in all reports, the country of Kenya does not have any social safety net, and it is up to Mama and her team to raise all the funds necessary to feed, clothe, educate and house these children!

Although there was no team from Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) visiting in 2025, our board member, Jodie, was able to visit in August and attend the wedding of Ezekiel, a Huruma alumnus, who has been part of her life since he was a child living in the home.

Jodie had a wonderful visit with Mama and the children and reported that everything appeared well.

Also, in 2025, Mama informed us that she has begun building the beehives that Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) sent funds for in 2024. She hired a local expert and the pictures she sent showed the platforms built as they first have to attract the bees… and then the hives will be built.

Members of the board were also able to speak about Huruma throughout the year, raising awareness and funds for the home.

In the fall of 2025, plans were put in place to have board members visit Huruma in March 2026. The plan for this visit will be to coincide with board members of Hope for Huruma (American charity supporting Huruma) visiting as well and to hold coordinating meetings with Mama, Sialo and staff. Our plan as well is for our board members to be able to meet with the Ministry of Children's Services around proposed changes that Kenya is looking at implementing regarding child welfare.

In closing, we continue to celebrate the successes of Huruma Children's Home and look forward to continuing our support of the home and the children. It continues to be our hope that you will continue to support these children as well through your donations to Huruma Children's Trust (Canada).

As mentioned in previous reports, Huruma Children's Home and Mama both have Facebook pages, and accounts on YouTube. You can check these sites and see what is going on daily in the lives of the children.

Respectfully submitted,
Board of Directors

Report

January 2025

Happy New Year!

We are happy to report that 2024 was a very busy year for Huruma Children's Trust (Canada). As supporters know, Huruma Children's Trust (Canada)'s only mandate is to raise awareness and funds for Huruma Children's Home in Ngong, Kenya. Our board of directors continues to consist of only volunteers.

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) is happy to announce that we were able to donate $25,500.00 Canadian dollars to Huruma Children's Home in 2024. This translates into 2,293,602.50 Kenyan shillings. Thank you to everyone! If you are looking at our report dated January 2024, you will notice that although our donation to Huruma was $20,300.00 the exchange totalled 2,415,943.00 shillings. It speaks to our lower Canadian dollar and the lower Kenyan shilling exchange rate. Today one Canadian dollar is worth 89.95 Kenyan shillings and in January 2024 one Canadian dollar was worth 129.17.

Mama Zipporah and her daughter, Sialo, continue to work hard at providing for all of the children residing in the home. Currently there are 300+ children in residence. She also continues to have about 60 children from the local slums come daily for their education and their main meal of the day. Costs continue to remain extremely high globally, and as supporters know, Kenya does not have any social safety net and Mama continues to raise all of the monies needed to feed, clothe, educate and house the children in her care. Our Canadian dollar does not go as far now as it has in the past; even from last year it translates to 40 shillings less per dollar.

In March of 2024 we had a large team visit Huruma consisting of 3 teens, plus 5 retired professionals that brought lots of expertise to the home. They had set a goal for their visit of painting all of the children's dormitories and this was completed to the delight of everyone. Some pictures on the website do show that sometimes there appeared to be more paint on the painter than on the walls! Also, two of the volunteers came with basketball expertise, so they purchased a steel backboard for the basketball nets and put up proper nets with proper lines. Everyone was very excited about the repairs, knowing it would no longer be impacted by the weather.

While at Huruma, some volunteers visited the farm at Gatune. The new house was completed, and another smaller tin house was erected as well. This allows the youth in transition to work at the farm and gain farming/crop life skills. Also, during this time we found out that troops of baboons were raiding Mama's crops. She had already put up fencing, but they easily jumped that. She researched and found that baboons hate honey bees, so Mama wanted to put beehives around the parameters of the fence to protect the crops. Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) put out an email to our donors explaining the situation and very quickly we were able to raise enough money to purchase 37 beehives! The honey and wax will be wonderful side products. This project is still in its infancy.

Also, 2024 was the 10th anniversary of Huruma Children's Trust (Canada). Volunteers from over the years were glad to gather and celebrate in September. It was fun to share memories and stories with each other.

In closing, we celebrate our successes and look forward to our continuing support of Huruma Children's Home. It continues to be our hope that you consider Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) a recipient of your generosity.

As mentioned in previous reports, Huruma Children's Home and Mama Zipporah both have Facebook pages and accounts on YouTube. You can follow them and see what is happening in the daily lives of the children.

Respectfully,
Huruma Children's Trust (Canada)

Report

April 2024

By the end of 2023, a group of 8 volunteers were committed to visiting Huruma Children's Home from March–April 2024. The team consisted of retirees and teens.

They had a packed agenda while there: painting the dormitories of the 250 children, fixing the backboards of the basketball courts, repairing them with steel instead of wood, and making a quilt involving 56 of the children drawing on squares, then putting it together and hanging it in the baby room. And when they were not busy doing all of that they were busy engaging with all of the children. They were missed when they left.

Also, as the rains have resumed, Mama was keen to get the gardens at Gatune planted. A big problem has surfaced there with large troops of baboons (200–300 at a time) coming to raid the gardens. Mama has found out that baboons hate bees, so she is exploring having beehives put around the perimeter of the gardens. The beehives can be twofold… providing the bees, but also pollinating and making honey and producing wax, as secondary products. She is hoping then to plant cactus around the fencing as well.

Report

January 2024

Happy New Year!

As mentioned in our Board of Directors report in January 2023, Kenya was in the midst of a 2½-year drought. Rains finally came in February 2023!!! It was life-saving.

As supporters know, Huruma Children's Trust (Canada)'s only mandate is to raise awareness and funds for Huruma Children's Home in Ngong, Kenya. Our Board of Directors is made up of volunteers; therefore, there are no management fees.

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) is happy to report, thanks again to our wonderful supporters, that in 2023 we were able to donate $20,300.00 Canadian dollars to Huruma Children's Home. With today's exchange that is 2,415,943.60 Kenyan shillings. Thank you to everyone!

Mama Zipporah, her daughter Sialo and the entire Huruma team continue to work hard in providing for all of the children. The residents of the home now number approximately 250, and as we all know education is the path out of poverty throughout the world. She also has over 50 children a day come from the local slums for their education and their main meal.

Costs for everything remain extremely high globally, and supporters of Huruma know that Kenya does not have any social safety net. Therefore, it is up to Mama to raise all the funds necessary to clothe, feed, house and educate the children in her care.

Our Canadian dollar is now (January 2024) worth 119.08 Kenya shillings. This is devastating to the Kenyan people as it means everything continues to increase in price, but it is good for our donations as the Canadian dollar goes farther in helping Mama meet the home's ongoing needs.

Volunteers and board members visit Huruma bi-annually to maintain and strengthen relationships. During 2020 and 2022 we had the great opportunity of some of our volunteers having expertise in school administration. A mentoring relationship was developed with the school principal. Through Zoom, their conversations have focused on evolving instructional practices in the school as Kenya transitions to a curriculum similar to Ontario's.

Junior High (Classes 7, 8 and 9) was introduced at Huruma in January 2023. Also, the house at the Gatune farm has been completed. We spent a day there in 2022 helping lay cinder blocks, and were very impressed again by Mama's ability to always be thinking about the future and how best to meet the needs of her children and help them achieve personal success.

As well as providing food for her children, there is an educational component of the farm. She has the youth live and work there, developing skills in planting, maintaining the fields and harvesting the crops. This allows them to develop life skills they can take with them when they leave Huruma as young adults.

It continues to be our hope that you consider Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) as a recipient of your generosity.

Huruma Children's Home in Ngong, Kenya and Mama Zipporah both have Facebook pages and accounts on YouTube. You can follow them there and see what is happening in the lives of the children.

Respectfully,

Report

January 2023

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) began 2023 with $5,197.35 in the bank account. The year-end books were forwarded on February 28, 2024 to Charities Directorate, Canada Revenue Agency.

2023 donations from Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) to Huruma Trust Fund in Kenya: January 25th: $5,000.00; February 4th: $1,300.00; April 17th: $5,000.00; September 21st: $4,000.00; December 20th: $5,000.00, for a total of $20,300.00.

Since the charity was registered by the CRA in 2014 we have donated $171,150.00 to Huruma Trust Fund in Kenya. Since forming as a charity in April 2014, Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) has raised $177,385.63.

Our expenses totalled $200.50. This included accounting fees, and fees for maintaining our web names. The website fees were taken as an in-kind donation this year as the couple are strong believers in what Mama Zipporah is providing for the children. PayPal fees were $66.42 and bank service charges were $210.00. The increase in bank fees is due to the fact the bank increased its wire fees… from $25.00 per wire to $50.00 per wire. 2023 ended with $2,444.80 in the bank.

At the end of 2022 the drought was still continuing in Kenya. But, by the end of February 2023, the rains finally came, and over the spring planting was resumed on the farm at Gatune. The house has been completed at the farm. Therefore, there is more work being done there and there is a place to stay.

There were no volunteer trips through Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) in 2023, although planning is in place for a 2024 trip.

Over the years, the board has discussed adding members to our Board of Directors. We felt 2 new members were appropriate. This would allow more people sharing the stories and raising awareness and hopefully funds for Huruma Children's Home. On April 25, 2023 we passed a unanimous motion that Michael J. Kelly and Margaret E. Schreurs-Kelly become new members of Huruma Children's Trust (Canada)'s board of directors. Mike and Marg are married. This is unusual for boards, but due to Mike's sight disability and due to the fact they both are passionate about Huruma and have both been there twice, we felt confident in making this motion. A letter was attached to the CRA returns this year noting this.

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) board members did make contact with a national company, but the outreach was not successful in obtaining financial support. This outreach to Canadian companies will continue. A recommendation was made by a friend of Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) that we reach out to the Canadian Consulate in Kenya regarding Canadian companies doing business in Kenya. If this is successful, the goal will be to meet with some of these companies, to build relationships between the personnel in Kenya and Huruma Children's Home, and hopefully have these exciting relationships reverberate back to the Canadian head offices, so they may be interested in supporting Mama and her children through corporate donations!

Formalizing this contact began in 2023, with the hopes that it will come to fruition in 2024.

Mama Zipporah's health has remained good. She looks forward to travelling to America in 2024 and if she is successful in obtaining a Canadian visa, she will travel to Canada while she is here, with the plan that Hope for Huruma, the American charity that supports Mama and her children, will pay the return airfare.

In closing, 2023 was another successful year for Huruma Children's Trust (Canada).

Report

January 2022

Happy New Year!

At the start of 2021 we hoped, as did the whole world, that COVID would be well behind us at the beginning of 2022, but as we know that is not the case.

Many things remain the same in Kenya. COVID continues to be rampant and Mama continues to work hard at ensuring her children remain safe and well cared for.

She reports that most of her staff has received the vaccination. She also shared that it is the AstraZeneca vaccine that is prominent there. She herself has had to wait for her vaccination, due to her bone marrow transplant a few years ago… as she had to have all of her vaccinations from birth, all over again. But, Mama continues to heal and her health continues to improve. Her focus remains on the well-being of her children and her staff.

Due to COVID it remains very difficult for international volunteers to visit the home. As we know, it is volunteers that bring awareness about the home to the world, and funds to the home. Therefore, due to this, donations continue to be way down. She has noted this on her Facebook page and prays that donors will return.

The Kenyan shilling is very volatile against almost all first-world currencies. Currently, one Canadian dollar is only worth 90.52 Kenyan shillings. A few days ago, when I started to write this report, one Canadian dollar was worth 100 Kenyan shillings. As has been mentioned before in these reports, the cost of living in Kenya is extremely high. We have all noticed here in Canada, and it is the same there, but with unstable income. Food, along with other necessities, remain very expensive in Kenya.

As many of you know, Kenya does not have any social safety net. It is up to Mama to raise all of the monies needed to feed, house, clothe and educate the children in her care. It continues to be by social media that she connects with her supporters throughout the world and alerts them to the home's situation and the ongoing, often dire need, for ongoing donations.

Earlier in 2021, we received a plea from Mama that her well had run dry, requesting donations to aid in the drilling of a new well! As always, Canadian supporters were quick to respond and we were able to send a substantial donation to her to aid in this emergency. She reports this was very helpful and timely, but she is looking ahead and acknowledges that a deeper well will be necessary in the not too distant future.

A great benefactor, who supported Mama's gardens enthusiastically, made Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) the recipient of donations at the time of his death. These funds will go a great distance in getting the gardens back up and running when COVID allows people to gather. Mama has acknowledged that it will take special direction from strong supervisors to maintain the garden, and looks forward to building another small home for the young adults who have aged out of the structured programming and need skills and employment as they move forward in their lives.

A running farm will also aid in the reduction of food costs, and provide an income with the sale of excess produce/legumes to the public.

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) is happy to report, thanks to our wonderful supporters, in 2021 we were able to donate $17,500.00 Canadian dollars to Huruma. On today's exchange that is 1,585,412.28 Kenyan shillings!

Again, we like to reiterate that all monies raised by Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) go directly to Huruma Children's Home. There are no management fees. We are all volunteers.

It continues to be our hope that you consider Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) as a recipient of your generosity.
