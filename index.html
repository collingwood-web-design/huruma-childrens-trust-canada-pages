Huruma Children's Home & School

Huruma Children's Trust (Canada) is a registered Canadian charity based in Collingwood, Ontario. Donors across Canada support orphaned and vulnerable children at Huruma Children's Home & School in Ngong, Kenya. The Trust raises awareness and funds in Canada; the home itself is operated in Kenya by Huruma Trust Fund — a separate organization.

On the outskirts of Nairobi, many children are orphans or live in families unable to care for them. Huruma gives them a safe place to sleep, learn, eat together, and grow.

What began with Mama Zipporah and her family now includes dormitories, a school through Form 4 (Grade 12), a large dining hall, and a church that welcomes the wider community.

About Mama Zipporah

Mama Zipporah was born and raised in Kenya. With her husband, Pastor Isaac Kamau, she founded Huruma Children's Home to protect children who, like herself as a child, had nowhere else to turn.

Today more than 250 children live at Huruma full-time. Each school day, about 90 more children from the Ngong slums come for education and a main meal. For some of Kenya’s most vulnerable children, the home offers real hope — a warm, accepting place to heal, learn, and grow.

Huruma has expanded to include a soccer field, basketball court, volleyball court, and a playground donated by friends of the orphanage. Music classes in singing, keyboard, guitar, and drums also help children take part in worship and praise at the church.

Their stories

Every child at Huruma has a path worth knowing.

Arshavin

Admitted in 2016 after his father became too ill to care for him, Arshavin was reunited with his younger brother, who had arrived in 2014. He is in Grade 7 and is one of Huruma's strongest students — and the best in French.

Zippy

Zipporah — known as Zippy — came to Huruma at birth. Her mother grew up at the home and needed support; Zippy now has two half-siblings living there too. A sweet Grade 2 student, she loves singing and dancing.

Blessing

Blessing arrived in 2018 after Children's Services found she was neglected and malnourished. Her health recovered quickly. Now in kindergarten (PP2), she is learning well and loves dancing and playing with friends.
